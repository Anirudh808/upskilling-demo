# PAGE 2 — THE PROBLEM & THE LOOP

## The placement gap isn't a content problem. It's a proof problem.

Your students have never had more access to learning material. Free courses, YouTube, MOOCs, bootcamps. And yet placement officers report the same three failures every year:

**1. Completion means nothing.**
A certificate that says "watched 40 hours of video" tells a recruiter nothing. Recruiters know it, so they ignore it — and re-test every candidate from scratch. Your students' learning hours convert into zero hiring advantage.

**2. Non-CSE students are invisible.**
The entire upskilling industry optimised for software hiring. Ask a leading coding platform to assess whether a student can calculate Carnot efficiency, size an op-amp gain stage, or run an FEA study on a cantilever beam — it cannot. So ECE, EEE, and Mechanical students get handed a Python course and told to "switch to IT."

**3. You're flying blind until it's too late.**
Placement readiness gets discovered in the final semester, during the first campus drive, when there's no time left to fix it.

## The SkillBridge loop

> **LEARN → PRACTISE → BUILD → VERIFY → GET HIRED**

| Stage | What the student does | What the institution gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | Structured courses in their *own* discipline | Curriculum-aligned upskilling, not generic content |
| **Practise** | Solve problems graded objectively — code *and* engineering | Early, granular signal on who is struggling |
| **Build** | Ship a guided project with a real deliverable | Portfolio evidence, not just marks |
| **Verify** | Sit a proctored assessment | A defensible, integrity-checked score |

Each stage feeds the next. The verification score is only meaningful because the learning preceded it. The employability score is only credible because the verification was proctored. That chain is the product.

## Practice that works for a thermodynamics problem, not just a linked list

### Three assessment types, not one ●

Coding platforms have exactly one hammer: write a function, pass the test cases. That covers software roles and abandons everyone else. SkillBridge grades three formats:

**Coding** — a real editor with syntax highlighting and starter code in **JavaScript, Python, Java, and C++**, in a professional split-pane layout: problem statement, editor, and console, every divider draggable. 15 problems from *Two Sum* to *N-Queens* and *Median of Two Sorted Arrays*, with progressive hints. ●
*Automated execution and judging of submissions:* ◐ **in development.**

**Real use case:** *An ECE student preparing for a core-company drive practises Nyquist rate and op-amp gain problems and gets graded instantly, with the derivation shown when they slip. Today, that student's only option is a PDF question bank with an answer key at the back.*

# PAGE 5 — VERIFY & GET HIRED

## Verification: the difference between claiming and proving

### Proctored skill assessments ◐

Any platform can issue a certificate. The question a recruiter actually asks is: *was anyone watching?*

Students schedule an assessment by **skill, difficulty tier (Basic / Intermediate / Advanced), and date and time** — a real booking flow, persisted. Before starting, they clear a readiness checklist. During the assessment, the platform runs live integrity checks:

- **Live camera presence** throughout the session ●
- **Tab-switch and focus-loss detection** — leaving the window is flagged, timestamped, and attached to the attempt ●
- **A hard countdown** with auto-submit on expiry ●
- **A violation log** delivered alongside the score, so a flagged attempt is visible rather than silently passed ●

**Status:** The proctoring engine, scheduling, timing, and violation logging are live. The full per-skill question banks and certificate issuance are ◐ **in development.**

### Verified credentials ○

**Planned.** Each passed assessment issues a certificate carrying the skill, tier, score, issue date, holder, and a unique authenticity ID that a recruiter can independently check — so the credential means something outside our platform.

**Real use case:** *Your placement cell tells a recruiter "our students are React-ready." The recruiter shrugs — every college says that. Instead: 34 students hold an Intermediate React credential, proctored, scored, integrity-logged, independently checkable. That is a conversation that ends in a campus drive.*
